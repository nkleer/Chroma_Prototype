## In short

This draft has **173 titles** (143 roles of the five commitment kinds and 30 statuses) and **165 perks** for modern Earth. 100 of the titles are yours, from "Modern Earth: 100 More Titles" (12:05); they are listed on their own under *Titles, volume 2*, with your profiles and turning points word for word. Shares, third profiles (marked) and how each is gained and lost are ours.

- A **title** is a role or status other people recognise, held for a stretch of life: a nurse, a wife, a parent of three, a union rep, a churchgoer, or a status such as graduate, veteran, widowed, someone with a record or in recovery. One title of each commitment kind is held at a time; statuses add up.
- A **perk** is something a person can do or has access to that changes their odds: a skill, a credential, standing, a bond with someone, or an asset. If they lose access, they keep the skill, and it fades slowly. A struck-off nurse still knows nursing.
- **Realistic numbers.** Each entry carries the share of people in a rich country who ever hold it. The shares add up to **18.4 titles** and **25.0 perks** over an average life. Research puts it at 10 to 20 titles and 15 to 30 perks (about 12.7 jobs from 18 to 56, US BLS; about seven roles held at once, Thoits 1983). Shares with a well-known figure name their source in the data file; the rest are marked as estimates.
- **Statuses carry no colors.** A status is a fact about a life, not a role with expectations: a degree, a divorce, a record, a stretch of homelessness. Only the roles of the five commitment kinds expect colors, and those become the commitment's profile. A status works only through what it brings and costs while held.
- **Color-even.** Each profile counts once, a pair half to each color (titles without profiles count their colors once). Over the 143 roles, every color sums to 54.50 to 55.00. Over the perks, every color sums to 32.83 to 33.17. Every commitment kind and every perk kind holds all five colors, and enemy pairs appear next to ally pairs and triads. No color is the good one. The boxer's training is Red discipline, the single parent's provision Black care, the farmer's growing herd Green ambition, the stepparent's slow patience Blue care, and the union rep's stand White freedom.
- **Safety.** Dark statuses are named plainly: a record, prison, a gang, homelessness, a soldier who has killed in war. Nothing touches sexual violence, nothing makes harming children a role or a skill, and nothing is graphic.

Situation names from the Earth library are in *italics*. They show where a title or perk can be gained or lost. The Library's next pass puts `title:`, `grants:`, `takes:` and `requires:` on those acts.

## How the engine reads it (engine answers, 08:55)

- **Titles at once.** Career, partner, children and faith hold one title each. Community can hold up to three at once (on the team, a scout and a prefect; a party member and an activist), mixed into one community commitment. Child roles are community titles.
- **What a title brings.** Its effects add to what the commitment kind already brings. A later title of the same kind replaces the earlier one. Statuses add up.
- **Two sorts of perk.** Skills, standing and bonds make acts in their ways go better. Credentials and assets (a licence, a passport, a car, savings, a house) work only where an act needs them.
- **Engine-granted statuses.** *widowed*, *retiree* and *newcomer* are named so the story uses these words. *newcomer* fades after two years; *immigrant* is a separate status for life.
