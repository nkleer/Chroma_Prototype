# Part 1. The five colors, in Rosewater's terms

The one-line core (MTG Wiki and Rosewater's framing): White, peace through order and structure; Blue, perfection
through knowledge; Black, power through opportunity; Red, freedom through action; Green, acceptance through wisdom
(growth through acceptance). Each color has a goal (end) and a method (means), and each color's greatest strength
is also its weakness pushed too far (Green's line in My Words: Green, "your greatest weakness is your greatest
strength pushed too far").

## White (W)

Sources: Great White Way (2003) and Revisited (2015); My Words: White (2025); https://mtg.wiki/page/White ;
Mechanical Color Pie 2017/2021.

- Goal: peace, a world without unnecessary suffering, where everyone gets along and the group prospers together.
  "White wants what is best for the whole" (2003).
- Means: structure. Two kinds of law: moral law (right and wrong are clear; religion, conscience, guilt, honor
  teach people to feel pride in goodness and shame in evil) and civil law (government, courts, police, the military
  give immediate consequences, a loss of freedom or resources). People respond to incentives and will do right if
  nudged; education and community belonging do the nudging. "The answer is simple: structure" (My Words: White).
- Values: community, cooperation, self-sacrifice, duty, honor, chivalry, honesty, loyalty, fairness and equality,
  charity, defense of the weak ("champion of the little guy", MCP 2021), order, discipline, faith and religion,
  civilization, medicine, construction, optimism, light (exposing what is hidden), planning and strategy.
- Fears and hates: selfishness and recklessness, the two forces that undo structure; lawbreakers; chaos.
- Strength: organization; many small pieces coordinated become stronger than any individual; given time to plot
  and plan it can solve most problems; opponents lose if they must play by White's rules.
- Flaws: inflexibility (it adapts only to what it predicted); disregard for the individual ("loses sight of the
  individual"); harshness and a heavy hand; treating small infractions like big ones; seeing in black and white;
  distrust of change and outsiders (wiki: xenophobia); it will imprison rather than kill, so threats return; a
  White villain is sure his harsh means build a better world.
- Archetypes Rosewater names: Superman, King Arthur, Captain Picard, Brienne of Tarth, Jim Gordon, Marge Simpson;
  villains Ozymandias and Alexander Pierce.
- How White sees the others: Blue (ally) shares careful thought, discipline and rules, and love of civilization,
  but cares more for the individual's perfection than the group's, and shares resources less fairly. Green (ally)
  shares community and knowing your role, but trusts wild instinct too much and lacks restraint. Black (enemy) is
  selfish, immoral and cruel, "the champion of selfishness", a cancer to remove. Red (enemy) is chaos personified,
  reckless, ignores law, unpredictable.

## Blue (U)

Sources: True Blue (2003) and Revisited (2015); My Words: Blue (2025); https://mtg.wiki/page/Blue ; MCP 2017/2021.

- Goal: perfection, of the self and of the world, through constant improvement; at the limit, omniscience ("he who
  knows all has no weakness", 2003). Each person is born a blank slate and must learn what to become; potential is
  unlimited.
- Means: knowledge and information. Study, schools, libraries, laboratories, teachers (whom Blue reveres), reading,
  discussion, experience; tools and technology to overcome limits; careful planning; taking time to see all the
  consequences of a choice. Information is also a tool for control, protection, and a weapon: secrets, illusion,
  trickery, misdirection, and exploiting the rules ("if the rules allow it, blue has no problems exploiting the
  system", 2003).
- Values: intellect, logic, mind over instinct, patience, foresight, curiosity, invention, innovation, possibility,
  individual paths to excellence (each person their own route), meritocracy of skill, efficiency, control.
- Fears and hates: impulse and recklessness that destroy years of work in a moment; people who will not take the
  time to think; determinism (being told your genes or birth fix who you are); tradition and ceremony for its own
  sake; stagnation; oversimplification.
- Strength: it can out-think anyone; given enough time it beats any opponent; it understands consequences.
- Flaws: passivity, hesitation, paralysis by perfectionism; reactive rather than proactive; slow, so a fast
  opponent wins before Blue has assessed the situation; coldness, social and emotional blindness; manipulation;
  arrogance of the mind.
- Archetypes: Merlin, Spock, Hermione Granger, Lisa Simpson, Sherlock Holmes, Mr. Fantastic, Hiro, Sheldon Cooper.
- How Blue sees the others: White (ally) shares planning, long-term thinking, teaching and hard work, but sends
  everyone down the same path ("I want everyone to find their own unique path", My Words: Blue) and clings to
  tradition. Black (ally) agrees that choice makes identity and knowledge is a weapon, but is reckless for quick
  wins and lets some shine at others' expense. Red (enemy) is snap decisions and emotion over logic; Blue grants it
  creativity and leaps of thought. Green (enemy) is "beholden to your genes", stuck in the past, anti-tool; Blue
  grants that nature's evolved solutions are worth studying.

## Black (B)

Sources: In the Black (2004) and Revisited (2015); My Words: Black (2025); https://mtg.wiki/page/Black ;
MCP 2017/2021.

- Goal: power, the ability to do whatever you want on your own terms; at the limit, omnipotence. In its own voice
  (2025) Black wants "every person to have the opportunity to thrive", and says you are worthy of the best.
- Means: opportunity, using whatever works. No taboos, no hesitation: bargains, leverage, ambition, sacrifice
  (paying a price, giving up something you valued for something greater), parasitism (siphoning others' resources),
  fear, deceit, manipulation, death and decay as tools. A five-step method (My Words: Black): know what you want;
  find the steps; list the obstacles; list your resources; use the resources on the obstacles.
- Values: self-interest ("If you don't look out for yourself, who will?"), self-determination, independence, honest
  realism about the world as it is (Black sees itself as the only realistic color), merit and reward for
  achievement, competence, taking responsibility for your own choices, calculated risk.
- Fears and hates: White's "good of the group" morality, which it calls a story told to make people act against
  their own interest; guilt; Green's destiny that forbids some ambitions; people who sacrifice themselves for others
  ("idiots, but very dangerous ones", 2004).
- Not evil: Rosewater insists Black is amoral, not immoral, "just as likely to commit moral acts as immoral ones";
  selfishness has legitimate uses.
- Strength: it never shuts off an option; it sees opportunities others miss; detachment and focus.
- Flaws: its willingness to do anything is self-destructive ("black's greatest enemy is ... itself"); risks
  backfire; it is the most isolated color, so it has no friends when trouble comes; distrust, betrayal, greed;
  life seen as zero-sum.
- Archetypes: Voldemort, Ramsay Bolton, Fish Mooney, Reverse Flash, Sarah Manning, Rocket Raccoon, Bart Simpson.
- How Black sees the others: White (enemy) is good at organizing and rhetoric but runs the morality "scam". Blue
  (ally) is smart, strategic, underhanded "in the most positive way", but too cautious and wants the ideal answer
  instead of the one that works. Red (ally) acts, doesn't complain, removes obstacles and enjoys life, but is
  reckless and short-sighted. Green (enemy) understands death and ferocity, but its "destiny" forbids ambition and
  it calls a bear killing a stag natural while condemning human ambition.

## Red (R)

Sources: Seeing Red (2004) and Revisited (2015); My Words: Red (2025); https://mtg.wiki/page/Red ; MCP 2017/2021.

- Goal: freedom, for itself and everyone, to live as the heart wants and to experience everything life offers, so
  that at the end you have no regrets and a life full of stories.
- Means: action, now. Two steps (My Words: Red): listen to what your body and heart tell you, then act on it
  instead of being frozen by fear or overthinking. Remove what blocks you ("knock it out of the way", 2004). Speed,
  persistence, fast resources spent now, risk, mischief, gambling.
- Values: emotion as a compass (joy, grief, rage, love, lust for life), passion, impulse, spontaneity, art, poetry,
  music, humour and fun, loyalty to friends and family it loves, honesty of feeling, courage, living in the moment,
  learning by doing and by mistakes. "If red is happy, it celebrates. If red is sad, it cries" (2004).
- Fears and hates: rules ("hates, hates, hates them", 2004), being told how to live, boredom and frustration,
  inaction and regret, a life spent waiting. "Red would much rather fail miserably than sit back and never try"
  (Revisited).
- Not stupid and not just a bully (2004): Red can be very smart; it holds the gentle sides of passion, art and
  drive; emotions are complex.
- Strength: clarity of desire and momentum; it acts before others have decided.
- Flaws: short-sightedness, no long game; impulsive mistakes and trouble; temper, violence; self-destruction;
  hard to counsel; easily manipulated through its emotions.
- Archetypes: Homer Simpson, Mabel Pines, the Human Torch, Quicksilver, Helena (Orphan Black).
- How Red sees the others: White (enemy) "loves telling everyone else how to live their life" and calls passion
  selfish. Blue (enemy) is "the mastermind of not acting", afraid of risk, treating feelings as dangerous. Black
  (ally) knows your needs matter, takes risks and has fun, but is too selfish, cannot trust, and will betray. Green
  (ally) prefers doing to thinking and trusts instinct, but its web-of-life spirituality and community feel alien
  and it is boring when calm.

## Green (G)

Sources: It's Not Easy Being Green (2002) and Revisited (2015); My Words: Green (2025); https://mtg.wiki/page/Green
; MCP 2017/2021.

- Goal: acceptance, and growth inside the natural order. The world is already right as it is ("We don't have to
  fix the world. It's perfect as it is", My Words: Green). Each being is born with what it needs for its role.
- Means: wisdom and acceptance. Four steps (My Words: Green): accept nature as a perfect whole; discover your
  innate gifts; find your calling by trial and error; fulfil it and help others find theirs. Instinct, intuition,
  observation, tradition and the ancestors, patience (change takes lifetimes), and nature's own strength when
  roused.
- Values: life and growth, interdependence ("everything is connected"), community and kin, roots and origin
  (where you come from shapes who you are), tradition and ancestral wisdom, instinct and spirit, reality over
  illusion, honest strength over trickery, balance, the cycle of life and death, responsibility to the whole web.
- Fears and hates: the unnatural and artificial; selfishness that "grows like a weed, strangling everything";
  disrupting the balance; losing one's nature to outside influence; disconnection from one's purpose.
- Strength: certainty; an unshakable belief and connection to the whole; raw strength and numbers.
- Flaws: complacency and fatalism ("it is meant to be"); resisting change and new tools; overconfidence; putting
  all eggs in one basket; easily fooled by subtlety; can be brutal (survival of the fittest, might makes right).
- Archetypes: Tarzan, King Kong, Swamp Thing, Wolverine, Groot, Owen Grady, Daryl Dixon, Hodor, Maggie Simpson.
- How Green sees the others: White (ally) understands community and faith beyond the material, but treats everyone
  the same, trusts law and cities over nature, and substitutes religion for spirit. Red (ally) listens to the body
  and lives in the present, but has no spiritual depth, cannot sit still, and its community stops at its friends.
  Blue (enemy) demands proof for everything, treats people as blank slates, and warps nature; they share a love of
  exploring and classifying. Black (enemy) glorifies selfishness and uses death as a tool; Green grants it knows
  life's dark side and admires its persistence.

## The ten core arguments between colors

From Thank You for Being a Friend (2017) https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/thank-you-being-friend-2017-03-20
and Pie Fights (2016) https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/pie-fights-2016-11-14 :
- Ally pairs share an enemy: WU "restraint" (against Red's impulse), UB "choice" (against Green's fixed roles),
  BR "individualism" (against White's group first), RG "intuition" (against Blue's intellect), GW "responsibility"
  (against Black's selfish exploitation).
- Enemy pairs argue one question each: W vs B, should society minimize pain (charity) or maximize motivation
  (merit)? U vs R, intellect or emotions? B vs G, can you change what life has planned for you? R vs W, freedom or
  security? G vs U, are we shaped by genetics or environment (nature or nurture)?
- Each ally pair splits on the question of the enemy pair it straddles: WU on free will vs destiny, UB on freedom
  vs security, BR on nature vs nurture, RG on group vs individual, GW on head vs heart.

# Part 2. Actions by color

Line format: act | means | ends ("–" = same as means) | life domain | stages.
Markers: [inner] an act of the mind or heart; [flaw] the color's flawed or wrong side (pickable, never preached).
Grounding: each list draws on the color's sources in Part 1, plus Colorful Replies (2018)
https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/colorful-replies-2018-07-16 and Return of Colorful Replies (2019)
https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/return-colorful-replies-2019-07-29 , where the colors answer everyday
questions (a stray cat: White returns it to its owner, Blue studies it, Black keeps it unless a reward is offered,
Red pets it, Green asks why anyone owns it; the dieter and the office cookies: White eats one to thank the baker,
Blue sticks to the diet, Black feigns disinterest and takes some later, Red eats as many as it can, Green eats if
hungry; best school memory: White elected to student government, Blue editor of the paper, Black beating Blue for
valedictorian, Red winning the state championship, Green homeschooled; love: White a bond where the whole is more
than its parts, Blue a mutually useful partnership, Black a tool that moves others, Red one of life's great
experiences, Green the bond with all life; retirement: Red does not plan an hour ahead).

# Part 4. Triads: shards and wedges

enemies, equivalently an ally pair plus the color both of them oppose. Rosewater wrote one article per triad, each
as a talk with the colors. Sources: Peace, Love and Understanding (Bant), Striving for Perfection (Esper), Looking
Out for Number One (Grixis), Following Your Heart (Jund), Searching Within (Naya), all 2008; We Will Survive

# Part 5. Four-color groups

A four-color group is named and defined by the one color it lacks: the four colors stand together against the fifth.
Sources: Guildpact (2006) printed the first four-color cycle, the five Nephilim (https://mtg.wiki/page/Nephilim ;
Rosewater interview https://magicuntapped.com/index.php/features/maro-talks-nephilim , where he says that in
hindsight he "would have made them legendary"). Rosewater, Howdy Partner (2016)
https://magic.wizards.com/en/articles/archive/making-magic/howdy-partner-2016-10-24 : four-color cards are "very hard
to design" and tend to come out "very soup-y and without a clear definition", which is why Commander 2016 also used
partner pairs. The five named philosophies come from Ethan Fleischer, Designing Commander (2016 Edition)
https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/card-preview/designing-commander-2016-edition-2016-10-24 : the design team was
challenged to find "a similar 'Guild identity' for each four-color grouping", and each was built around what its four
colors share against the missing one. Names, commanders and Nephilim: https://mtg.wiki/page/Commander_2016 ,
https://mtg.wiki/page/Color . (Rosewater's own writing on four-color is about the design difficulty; the
philosophies below are Fleischer's, published on magic.wizards.com, not Rosewater's. mtg.wiki/page/Four-color
returned 403 and its fandom/Wayback copies were blocked.)
Means "W U B G" = an act using all four colors' tools; the missing color is the one the act refuses. For Chroma, a
four-color person is best read by the fifth color they push away.

# Part 6. Means versus ends

Grounding. Rosewater's 2015 Revisited series asks the same six questions of every color, and the first two split the
goal from the method: "What does the color desire? What is its end goal?" and "What means does the color use to
achieve these ends?" (True Blue Revisited https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/true-blue-revisited-2015-07-20 ).
So "White wants peace", reached "by creating rigid structure" (Great White Way Revisited
https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/great-white-way-revisited-2015-07-13 ); "Black wants power", reached
with "no taboos, no restrictions, no rules" (In the Black Revisited
https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/black-revisited-2015-07-27 ). The MTG Wiki compresses each color into
"X through Y", X the goal and Y the means (https://mtg.wiki/page/Color ), and adds the key rule for reading a
person: "An organized character is not automatically White"; "It is the value of these traits that defines a
character to a color, not the presence of them." In Pie Fights (2016)
https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/pie-fights-2016-11-14 Rosewater notes that "each color is
fundamentally fighting for the same thing against each of its enemies" (White and Black both want a better society:
White one with "no unnecessary suffering", Black one where those who work "reap the rewards"), so colors can share an
end and fight over the means. The Mechanical Color Pie 2017
https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/mechanical-color-pie-2017-2017-06-05 shows the same split on cards: an
effect can sit in two colors with "very different flavor rationales" (lifelink in White and Black; Black's card draw
must pay a cost, Blue's does not). Black's own voice states the crossover plainly: if you can help those who matter to
you, you should, "provided it doesn't conflict with your own needs" (My Words: Black
https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/my-words-black ).

For Chroma: the means column says which color's tools the hand used; the ends column says which color the heart
served. A person's color is read mainly from ends over a lifetime. An act whose means are the enemy of its end
(W>B, B>W, U>R, R>U, B>G, G>B, R>W, W>R, G>U, U>G) is where a character feels torn or is called a hypocrite.

# Part 7. What makes colors shift

Sources: the 2015 Revisited series, whose fourth question for every color is "What does the color despise? What
negatively drives the color?" (URLs in the master list); My Words: White, Blue, Black, Red, Green (2025); Pie Fights
(2016) https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/pie-fights-2016-11-14 ; Thank You for Being a Friend (2017)
https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/thank-you-being-friend-2017-03-20 ; Planeswalking Down Memory Lane
(2018) https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/planeswalking-down-memory-lane-2018-07-23 ; MTG Wiki
https://mtg.wiki/page/Color (sections on individual characters and influences) and the character pages
https://mtg.wiki/page/Ajani_Goldmane , /Nahiri , /Garruk , /Sorin , /Teferi , /Liliana , /Tezzeret , /Sarkhan ;
for Nissa, EDHREC, Every Color-Shifted Planeswalker, Ranked https://edhrec.com/articles/every-color-shifted-planeswalker-ranked
(secondary). Lines marked "For Chroma" are this file's reading of the sources, not claims made by them.

## How a color identity forms

- Value, not behaviour. "It is the value of these traits that defines a character to a color, not the presence of
  them"; "An organized character is not automatically White" (mtg.wiki/page/Color). A person may use any color's
  tools; their color is what they keep choosing to serve.
- Scale. A color's philosophy is written for groups; in one person it shrinks to the everyday: the wiki's example is a
  White individual who simply values the family eating together at the table and good manners (mtg.wiki/page/Color).
- Three influences. The wiki names bloodline, race and occupation as forces that colour a character; its example is a
  goblin in the Azorius Senate who "may be Blue/White, but will likely have a Red influence that will surface in their
  actions, words, responses, or thoughts". For Chroma: family of origin, culture and community, and the job.
- The colors' own theories of formation are themselves an argument (Pie Fights; Thank You for Being a Friend):
  Green says you are born with your nature and find your calling by experiment ("The key to figuring this out is trial
  and error", My Words: Green); Blue says every person "is born as a blank slate" and is made by education and lived
  experience ("It's one thing to know something, but it's a different thing to experience it", My Words: Blue); Black
  and Blue together say identity is a choice (UB, "choice"); White says you take the role the group needs; Red says
  your impulses show you who you are (the BR split on nature versus nurture). For Chroma: early colour can come from
  temperament (Green's view) and then move with experience (Blue's view); both are canon.

## How identity changes

- Dilution before conversion. "Typically, when an opposing influence surfaces, it serves only to dilute the
  character's other colors"; so a Black/Red character with a White influence reads as a softer Black/Red, not as
  White (mtg.wiki/page/Color). A new color first softens the old ones.
- Drift by company. People living among a color they do not hold may be "suffering under peer pressure", may be
  "losing sight of morality", may not be aware of what they are doing, or may be "reconsidering their views and making
  a shift into or toward the color" (mtg.wiki/page/Color). Surroundings pull; the pull can be resisted, unnoticed, or
  accepted.
- Strength pushed too far. Each color's greatest strength is also its weakness pushed too far (Part 1; My Words:
  Green). For Chroma: a shift often starts at a color's flaw (White's heavy hand, Blue's paralysis, Black's isolation,
  Red's regretted outburst, Green's fatalism) because the flaw is where life pushes back.
- On cards, Rosewater records the first color changes: Ajani "was the first planeswalker to get a second card and the
  first to change colors", and Garruk Relentless was "the first planeswalker that changes color within the card"
  (Planeswalking Down Memory Lane, 2018).

## What the planeswalker stories show

- Ajani (White; then Red-White; then Green-White). A White healer and protector. After his brother Jazal was
  murdered, Sarkhan "advised him to take hold of his anger and use it" to hunt the killer: grief turned to rage added
  Red (Ajani Vengeant). Time with Elspeth, and her death, "showed him that his fury wasn't the correct path to inner
  peace"; he lost Red "after learning that rage doesn't bring peace" and became White-Green, a mentor sworn to keep
  watch "until all have found their place" (mtg.wiki/page/Ajani_Goldmane). Pattern: violent loss pulls toward Red;
  grief worked through pulls toward Green.
- Nahiri (White; then Red-White). A guardian of Zendikar's prison; her call for help went unanswered and Sorin
  imprisoned her in the Helvault for about a thousand years. Freed, she found her home in ruins, and "Enraged, she
  swore vengeance against Sorin, vowing to make Innistrad bleed in the same way Zendikar had suffered"; she is now
  "primarily white-aligned, but secondary in red mana" (mtg.wiki/page/Nahiri). Pattern: betrayal by an ally plus long
  injustice lays Red vengeance over White duty.
- Garruk (Green; then Black-Green; then Green). Liliana used the Chain Veil and "a surge of black magic that cursed
  him"; under the curse he came to see all planeswalkers as prey, "losing the difference between being a hunter and a
  murderer". Cleansed of the curse, he lost his Black; helped by strangers, he said "They helped me. I will help
  them" (mtg.wiki/page/Garruk). Pattern: an outside corruption (in human terms: addiction, abuse, a poisonous
  environment) pulls Green toward Black; being cared for brings it back.
- Sorin (Black; then White-Black). A vampire who created the angel Avacyn to protect Innistrad's humans, and so also
  to stop the vampires destroying their own food supply, and was seen as a traitor by his kind
  (mtg.wiki/page/Sorin; Sorin, Lord of Innistrad is white-black). Pattern: long self-interest that becomes
  stewardship adds White; a living example of Black means serving a White end (Part 6).
- Teferi (Blue; then White-Blue). The wiki lists him as once blue-centred and now "primarily blue-aligned, but
  secondary in white mana" (mtg.wiki/page/Teferi). It does not state why; in the story the change comes after he gave
  up his spark to save his homeland's people and settled as a husband, father and teacher, and Teferi, Hero of
  Dominaria is white-blue. For Chroma: sacrifice and parenthood add White.
- Liliana (a healer's apprentice, then Black). A cleric studying the healing arts who secretly dabbled in necromancy
  to heal better; when her brother Josu fell ill, a necromantic cure went wrong and destroyed him, and her spark
  ignited in that moment; later she bound herself to demons for youth and power. In War of the Spark she broke
  Bolas's hold and Gideon took the fatal cost of her contract on himself, which set her on a path of responsibility
  (mtg.wiki/page/Liliana). Pattern: love plus desperation plus a forbidden shortcut pulls toward Black; someone else's
  sacrifice can open the way back.
- Tezzeret (Blue; then Blue-Black). After his mother's death he "swore he would become a mage someday so that nobody
  could take anything from him ever again"; inducted into Bolas's Infinite Consortium, he became "primarily
  blue-aligned, but secondary in black mana" (mtg.wiki/page/Tezzeret). Pattern: early loss and powerlessness, then a
  ruthless patron, pull Blue toward Black.
- Sarkhan (Red-Green; then Black-Red; then Blue-Red-Green). "Driven to find a dragon to worship, he was found by
  Bolas", who made him a pawn; "While his mind was broken by Bolas, he had access to black mana" (Sarkhan the Mad is
  black-red) (mtg.wiki/page/Sarkhan). After his journey into the past to save Ugin, his card Sarkhan Unbroken is
  blue-red-green. Pattern: serving someone who breaks you adds Black; a healed mind and a longer view add Blue.
- Nissa (Green; then Green-Blue). On Amonkhet she passed the trial of Kefnet, the god of knowledge, and Nissa, Steward
  of Elements is green-blue (EDHREC, secondary). Pattern: mastering a discipline from outside your tradition adds
  Blue.

## What pulls a person toward or away from each color

Grounded in each color's "What does the color despise? What negatively drives the color?" answer (Revisited, 2015)
and its own voice (My Words, 2025); the life examples are this file's reading.

White
- Toward: being carried by a community through a crisis; belonging ("You become part of something larger than
  yourself", My Words: White); seeing harm done by selfishness or recklessness, the two forces White fears most ("The
  two biggest dangers to our plans are selfishness and recklessness"); taking responsibility for others (a child, a
  team, a parish, an ageing parent); a shared effort that works; faith.
- Away: an institution that fails or abuses you; rules that only restrain ("I get how that can feel restricting at
  times", White admits); finding hypocrisy among the righteous (Black's charge that morality is a story told to make
  people act against their own interest); being judged harshly for a small fault.
- Temptation inside White: the heavy hand ("If we have to take a heavy hand to ensure peace ... it is what has to be
  done"), the road to the White villain who is sure harsh means build a better world (Part 1).

Blue
- Toward: curiosity rewarded; a good teacher; a hard problem solved by preparation; a costly mistake made on impulse
  ("In a moment, you can take actions that can have lifelong consequences", My Words: Blue); being told you can
  become anything, the blank-slate promise.
- Away: a chance missed while you were still thinking (Red's charge that Blue is "the mastermind of not acting",
  Part 1); knowledge that could not save someone; a feeling ignored until it broke something; being told your genes
  decide who you are ("Green believes that you're beholden to your genes").
- Temptation inside Blue: control through information, secrecy and manipulation (the road to Blue-Black); coldness.

Black
- Toward: being let down, cheated or abandoned by the group; loss and powerlessness (Tezzeret's vow after his
  mother's death); being told "no" by rules or by "destiny"; seeing that hesitation cost you ("The biggest problem
  most people have is their unwillingness to capitalize on opportunity", My Words: Black); scarcity; a win gained by
  bending a rule; a shortcut offered when you are desperate (Liliana).
- Away: isolation when trouble comes (Black is the most isolated color, Part 1); a betrayal that comes back to you;
  someone who sacrifices for you (Gideon for Liliana); being cared for with nothing asked in return (Garruk).
- Fear inside Black: being controlled or owing anyone; Black names guilt a tool others use to bind you.

Red
- Toward: rules that stifle ("White loves telling everyone else how to live their life", My Words: Red); boredom and
  frustration, which Red despises (Seeing Red Revisited); the fear of an unlived life ("did you make the effort to
  maximize the time you had?"); a brush with death; injustice that makes you angry; grief that turns to rage (Ajani,
  Nahiri); falling in love.
- Away: an outburst that hurts someone you love; consequences catching up; rage that brings no peace (Ajani lost Red
  "after learning that rage doesn't bring peace"); the long duties of parenthood or care.
- Temptation inside Red: acting without thought until it becomes self-destruction (Part 1).

Green
- Toward: discovering what you are naturally good at; time in nature, with roots and kin; acceptance after a loss
  ("Unlike the other colors, I'm not afraid of death", My Words: Green); ageing; burnout from striving ("we created
  those problems"); the comfort of "You are perfect just the way you are".
- Away: an injustice that acceptance would leave standing; ambition beyond your station; proving wrong someone who
  said your nature limits you; moving far from your roots; a life built on tools and screens.
- Temptation inside Green: fatalism and complacency ("it is meant to be", Part 1).

## The inner conflicts: enemy arguments inside one person

Rosewater: "each color is fundamentally fighting for the same thing against each of its enemies" (Pie Fights,
2016). Inside one person the two enemies want the same stake and argue about how. Each pair below gives the shared
stake and question (Pie Fights), the inner voice, common life triggers, and two [inner] acts in Part 2's format.

White vs Black. Stake: a good society. Question: minimise pain (charity) or maximise motivation (merit)? Inner voice:
"Should I help, or look after my own?" Triggers: a friend asks for money; a bonus you could share; reporting a
colleague who once covered for you; paying for a parent's care.
- give to the collection, then quietly resent it [inner] | W | B | money, meaning | adult, mature
- keep the promise even though breaking it would pay [inner] | W | – | friends, work | all

Blue vs Red. Stake: a fulfilled self. Question: intellect or emotion? Inner voice: "Think it through, or just go?"
Triggers: a proposal; a job offer abroad; a sudden crush; a fight you could walk away from.
- write a list of pros and cons, then ignore it and follow your heart [inner] | U | R | love, meaning | juv, ya, adult
- count to ten while your temper begs to speak [inner] | U | – | conflict | all

Black vs Green. Stake: your own life. Question: can you change what life has planned for you? Inner voice: "Accept
my lot, or take more?" Triggers: a serious diagnosis; the family trade you are expected to take on; a ceiling of
class or place; ageing.
- refuse the diagnosis and fight it with everything you have [inner] | B | – | health | adult, mature, elder
- make peace with a limit you once swore to beat [inner] | G | – | meaning, aging | mature, elder

Red vs White. Stake: the right shape of society. Question: freedom or security? Inner voice: "My freedom or my
duty?" Triggers: a first child; a strict job; a wedding; a law you think unjust.
- stay in the safe job and dream every night of quitting [inner] | W | R | work, meaning | adult, mature
- break a rule because your conscience says so, and accept the punishment [inner] | R | W | conflict, faith | juv, ya, adult

Green vs Blue. Stake: becoming yourself. Question: nature or nurture? Inner voice: "Is this who I am, or who I could
become?" Triggers: a career change; therapy; a child's talents; a treatment that would change your body.
- wonder whether you are your father's temper or your own choices [inner] | U G | – | family, meaning | juv, ya, adult
- decide you were simply made this way, and stop fighting it [inner] | G | – | meaning | ya, adult, mature

Ally pairs also split inside one person, each on the question of the enemy pair it straddles (Thank You for Being a
Friend, 2017): White-Blue on free will versus destiny, Blue-Black on freedom versus security, Black-Red on nature
versus nurture, Red-Green on the group versus the individual, Green-White on head versus heart.

## Shift triggers for the engine

For Chroma (this file's reading, each line drawn from the evidence above): trigger, then the usual pull.
- a violent or unfair loss of someone loved → toward R (rage), then toward G if grief is worked through (Ajani)
- betrayal or abandonment by an ally or an institution → toward R (revenge) or B (never trust again) (Nahiri,
  Tezzeret; White's "away" list)
- powerlessness in childhood (poverty, a loss no one prevented) → toward B: vowing, like Tezzeret, that "nobody could take anything
  from him ever again"
- a desperate love and a forbidden shortcut → toward B (Liliana)
- a corrupting influence or a ruthless patron → toward B, first as dilution, then as drift (Garruk, Sarkhan; wiki on
  peer pressure)
- being cared for, freely, by others → back toward the old color and toward W or G (Garruk)
- someone's sacrifice for you → away from B, toward W (Liliana and Gideon)
- parenthood, stewardship, responsibility for others → toward W (Teferi, Sorin)
- a costly impulsive mistake → toward U and W (My Words: Blue; WU "restraint")
- a chance lost to overthinking, boredom, a brush with death → toward R (My Words: Red)
- mastering a discipline, a good teacher, a new field of study → toward U (Nissa, Sarkhan)
- finding your natural gift by trial and error, a return to roots, ageing → toward G (My Words: Green)
- the hypocrisy of the righteous, a rule that crushes you → away from W, toward R or B
- success won by bending a rule → toward B; the same success punished → back toward W
- a long life among people of one color → that color's influence first dilutes, then may become a real shift
  (mtg.wiki/page/Color)
